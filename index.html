Case study 4 · Personal project, Nov 2026

Turning delivery data into decisions

I analyzed 99K+ orders from a Brazilian e-commerce marketplace in SQL, then designed a one-page dashboard so a non-technical manager can see where revenue comes from and what hurts customer satisfaction.

Role

Analyst and dashboard designer. Solo project.

Question

Where does revenue come from, and what hurts customer satisfaction?

Data

Public Olist dataset: 99K+ orders across 9 related tables, 2016 to 2018.

Tools

PostgreSQL, DBeaver, Power BI

Late deliveries hurt ratings

8% of orders arrive late and average a 2.57 review score, against 4.29 for on-time orders.

Retention is the opportunity

Only 3% of customers buy a second time, and the top 10% of customers drive 38% of revenue.

Fix the biggest sources first

Northeast states have the highest late rates (15 to 23%), and Rio de Janeiro has the most late orders by volume.

Findings show association, not causation. Queries, assumptions and recommendations are in the GitHub repo.

Case study 1 · In progress

Designing trust into AI summaries

Clinicians can’t act on a summary they can’t trust, so I’m building an LLM pipeline that checks every summary before a person sees it, and fails safely when a check fails.

1. Feature enginePulls structured patient features.
2. RAG retrievalFinds supporting evidence.
3. LLM draftWrites the summary.
4. Rule-based verifierChecks the draft against source data.
5. Template fallbackUses a safe template if a check fails.
